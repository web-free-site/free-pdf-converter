To compress a PDF to 2MB, add your file below, pick the Low level for the best image quality, and click Compress PDF. The target size is already set to 2 MB, a budget that lets you keep high image quality for portfolios and reports, and the compression runs entirely on your device.

Quality-first compression

With 2MB to work with, you rarely need destructive settings. The Low level removes dead weight, such as duplicate fonts, unused objects, and editing leftovers, and keeps images at high quality, which suits design portfolios, photography-heavy reports, and print-ready documents that still have to pass an upload check.

Common 2MB situations

University thesis submission systems, conference paper uploads, and many procurement portals cap files at 2MB. Documents exported from Word or Google Docs almost always fit at the Low level; for InDesign or Canva exports with full-resolution imagery, the 2 MB target lowers image quality only as far as it has to.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which level keeps the best image quality?

Lossless keeps images exactly as they are, and Low keeps them at high quality. With the 2 MB target set, Hyper Compress starts from the level you pick and lowers image quality only if the file doesn't fit.

Why is my exported PDF so large in the first place?

Design tools often embed full-resolution images and complete font files. Compression subsets the fonts and re-encodes images at sensible resolution, which is where the large savings come from.

Can I check the result before downloading?

You see the compressed size immediately after processing, and you can re-run with a different level or target as many times as you like. Nothing is uploaded, so iteration is instant.
